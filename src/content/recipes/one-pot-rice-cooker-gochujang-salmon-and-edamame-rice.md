# One-Pot Rice Cooker Gochujang Salmon and Edamame Rice

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Standard stovetop salmon dries out in a heartbeat if you look away for two minutes. A rice cooker solves this by trapping saturated steam in an enclosed chamber, gently poaching the salmon while the grains cook below. As the fish steams, seasoned fats and sweet-spicy gochujang drip directly into the rice, flavoring every bite without extra pans. You get tender, flaked fish and perfectly cooked rice with zero active monitoring.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Rice Cooker Size**: Any standard 4-cup to 10-cup (uncooked capacity) rice cooker works. A basic single-switch "cook/warm" model works just as well as an expensive fuzzy-logic unit.
- **Rinse the Rice Well**: Wash the grains in cold water until the runoff is mostly clear. Stripping that excess surface starch prevents the bottom from turning into gummy, burnt paste.
- **Dry the Fish**: Pat the salmon fillets bone-dry with paper towels before brushing on the sauce. If the fish is wet, the glaze slides right off into the cooking water instead of caramelizing onto the flesh.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Jasmine or medium-grain white rice**: 360g (2 US cups) – *Avoid parboiled or minute rice; they turn to mush.*
- **Water or low-sodium chicken broth**: 450ml (scant 2 cups) – *Slightly less than the standard 1:1 ratio because the fish releases water as it cooks.*
- **Salmon fillets**: 450g (1 lb), skinless or skin-on – *Cut into 2 to 4 even fillets, about 3/4-inch to 1-inch thick.*
- **Frozen shelled edamame**: 150g (1 cup) – *Add them straight from the freezer; do not waste time thawing.*
- **Gochujang (Korean chili paste)**: 2 tbsp (30g) – *Look for the red tub in the international aisle.*
- **Soy sauce**: 1.5 tbsp (22ml) – *Use regular or low-sodium, not dark soy sauce.*
- **Toasted sesame oil**: 1 tbsp (15ml)
- **Honey or brown sugar**: 1 tbsp (15g) – *Balances the sharp heat and fermented funk of the gochujang.*
- **Garlic**: 2 cloves (6g), finely minced
- **Fresh ginger**: 1 tsp (3g), grated or finely minced
- **Scallions & toasted sesame seeds**: 2 stalks, sliced – *Optional, for finishing.*

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Prep the Rice Base**: Rinse the 360g of rice in cold water 3 to 4 times until the water runs mostly clear. Drain thoroughly in a sieve, dump into the inner pot, and pour in 450ml of water or broth. Level the surface flat with a spatula.

2. **Mix the Glaze**: In a small bowl, whisk together the gochujang, soy sauce, sesame oil, honey, minced garlic, and grated ginger until completely smooth. Divide the sauce: set 1 tablespoon aside for serving.

3. **Layer the Pot**: Scatter the frozen edamame evenly over the top of the raw rice. Pat your salmon fillets dry with a paper towel, then brush the top and sides generously with the remaining glaze. Set the fillets directly on top of the edamame and rice. Do not push them down into the liquid; let them sit on the surface.

4. **Cook**: Close the lid and hit the standard **White Rice** or **Cook** cycle (typically **30 to 35 minutes**). 

5. **Mid-Point Rule**: Leave the lid shut. Do not pop it open to check. Opening the lid dumps the trapped steam, drops the inner chamber temperature by up to 20°F, and guarantees unevenly cooked rice.

6. **Check for Doneness**: When the machine flips to "Warm", let it rest closed for **5 minutes** to finish steaming. Open the lid. The salmon should flake apart easily under gentle fork pressure and reach an internal temperature of **135°F to 145°F (57°C to 63°C)** in the thickest part. 

7. **Finish and Serve**: Drizzle the reserved glaze over the salmon. Use a rice paddle to break the salmon into large chunks, folding it gently into the rice and edamame so the seasoning distributes evenly. Top with sliced scallions and sesame seeds.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **The rice turns out wet and mushy**: You added standard amounts of water. Salmon releases fat and moisture while it steams. **The fix**: Always scale back the water by 2 to 3 tablespoons compared to what you would use for plain rice.
- **The bottom of the pot scorches**: Gochujang contains sugar, which burns fast if it touches the hot bottom of the pot. **The fix**: Keep the glaze entirely on the surface of the salmon. Never stir the sauce into the raw rice before cooking.
- **The salmon is chalky and overcooked**: Your fillets were too thin (under 1/2-inch). **The fix**: Buy uniform, center-cut fillets. If you only have thin tail pieces, tuck the thin edges under themselves to create a thicker piece so they do not overcook.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Salmon swap**: Don't waste money buying fresh salmon if prices are sky-high. Boneless, skinless chicken thighs cut into bite-sized chunks cook in the exact same 30-minute window. Canned tuna can also be folded in at the very end (do not cook canned tuna for 30 minutes).
- **Edamame swap**: If edamame is hard to find or overpriced, use standard frozen sweet peas. Toss them in frozen right over the rice just like the edamame.
- **Gochujang swap**: If your pantry lacks gochujang, whisk 1.5 tablespoons of Sriracha with 1 teaspoon of brown sugar and 1 extra teaspoon of soy sauce. It won't have the same fermented depth, but it provides the right balance of sweet heat.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Store leftovers in an airtight glass container for up to **3 days**. 
- **Reheat in the Rice Cooker**: Never blast leftover salmon in the microwave on high—it turns rubbery and smells terrible. Instead, put the leftover rice and flaked salmon back into the rice cooker pot, sprinkle with **1 to 2 tablespoons of water**, close the lid, and turn on the **Warm** setting for **15 to 20 minutes** (or run a quick Cook cycle for **5 minutes** if your machine allows). The gentle steam brings moisture back to the rice without overcooking the fish.
