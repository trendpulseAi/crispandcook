# Viral Rice Cooker French Onion Ground Beef & Rice

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Most viral one-pot rice cooker meals end up either burnt at the bottom or drowning in grease. The fix is layering: the rinsed rice absorbs the liquid from the bottom up, while the seasoned beef cooks on top, rendering its fat through the grains as it steams. Using canned condensed French onion soup cuts out 45 minutes of stovetop onion caramelization and provides both the salt and the cooking liquid needed to cook the rice evenly. You get a savory, deeply savory beef-and-rice dinner in one machine with zero pans to scrub.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Appliance specifics**: Use a standard 6-cup (uncooked) or larger rice cooker. A basic one-button "cook/warm" model works just as well as a multi-function fuzzy-logic cooker. If your bowl has a worn, scratched nonstick coating, grease it lightly with neutral oil first or the onion sugars will stick.
- **Rinse the rice thoroughly**: Wash your jasmine or long-grain white rice in a fine-mesh strainer under cold water until the water runs clear (at least **3 rinses**). If you skip this, surface starch mixes with the condensed soup and turns into sticky glue.
- **Lean meat matters here**: Stick to 85/15 or 90/10 ground beef. Anything fattier (like 73/27) dumps too much grease into the pot, leaving the rice oily rather than fluffy.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Long-grain white rice**: 1.5 cups (285g) – standard long-grain or jasmine; avoid short-grain or sushi rice as they get gummy.
- **Ground beef (85/15 or 90/10)**: 1 lb (450g) – cold from the fridge is fine.
- **Condensed French onion soup**: 1 can (10.5 oz / 298g) – Campbell's or store brand; do not dilute with water yet.
- **Beef broth (low-sodium)**: 1 cup (240ml) – keep it low-sodium so you can control the salt level.
- **Worcestershire sauce**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – adds depth and balances the sweetness of the canned onions.
- **Garlic powder**: 1 tsp (3g) – distributes more evenly than fresh garlic in a sealed cooker.
- **Black pepper**: 1/2 tsp (1.5g) – freshly cracked is best.
- **Shredded Swiss or Gruyère cheese**: 1 cup (100g), optional – melts over the top immediately after cooking. Don't waste money on imported Gruyère if sliced deli Swiss is cheaper.
- **Green onions**: 2 stalks, finely sliced – for freshness at the end.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Prep and wash the base**: Rinse 1.5 cups of rice until the runoff is clear. Drain thoroughly and dump the wet rice directly into the bottom of the rice cooker bowl. Spread it into an even, flat layer.
2. **Mix the cooking liquid**: In a measuring cup, whisk together the canned French onion soup, 1 cup of beef broth, Worcestershire sauce, garlic powder, and black pepper. Pour the mixture directly over the rice. Do not stir—let the liquid naturally settle through the grains.
3. **Layer the beef**: Pinch the raw ground beef into small, bite-sized clusters (about marble-sized) and scatter them evenly across the surface of the rice and liquid. **Do not pack the meat into a solid patty**, or the steam won't pass through to cook the rice evenly.
4. **Run the cycle**: Close the lid and hit the standard **White Rice** cycle (or push the toggle down to **Cook**). This typically takes **30 to 35 minutes**. Leave the lid closed the entire time; opening it vents critical steam and lowers the internal pot temperature.
5. **Rest and check doneness**: When the cycle clicks over to **Warm**, let the pot sit untouched for **5 minutes**. Pop the lid: the beef should be cooked through with no pink remaining (minimum **160°F / 71°C** internally), and the liquid should be fully absorbed.
6. **Melt and fluff**: If using cheese, scatter it immediately over the hot surface, close the lid, and let it sit on **Warm** for **2 minutes** until completely melted. Use a rice paddle to fold the melted cheese, beef, and rice together, breaking up any meat clusters. Top with green onions and serve.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **The rice is crunchy in the center**: Your rice cooker clicked off early because the liquid evaporated too fast. Pour in **1/4 cup (60ml) hot beef broth or water**, close the lid, and run another **Cook** cycle for 5–7 minutes, or leave it on **Warm** for 10 minutes to finish steaming through.
- **The bottom layer scorched black**: French onion soup has a high sugar content from the onions. If your cooker runs hot, this sugar can caramelize too aggressively. Next time, add 2 tablespoons of extra broth and avoid using the "Quick Cook" mode, which uses higher, less-controlled base heat.
- **The dish is overly greasy**: You used beef with high fat content (like 80/20) without par-cooking. To fix the current pot, fold in 2 paper towels across the surface for 30 seconds to blot excess pool fat before stirring. For next time, buy 90/10 beef or brown and drain the meat in a skillet before adding it to the cooker.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Dry onion soup mix instead of canned**: If you don't have canned French onion soup, combine 1 packet of dry onion soup mix with 2 cups (480ml) of water and 1 tablespoon of butter.
- **Ground turkey or pork**: Ground turkey works well and is naturally leaner, but add 1 tablespoon of olive oil to the liquid so the meat doesn't dry out.
- **Frozen minced onions**: If your canned soup lacks enough onion texture, toss half a cup of cheap frozen diced onions directly into the rice layer before cooking.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Let leftovers cool completely, then seal them in an airtight glass or plastic container. Store for up to **4 days**.
- **Reheat in the Rice Cooker**: Don't microwave this if you want to keep the rice from drying out. Put the leftover beef and rice back into the rice cooker pot, sprinkle **2 tablespoons of water or beef broth** over the top to create steam, close the lid, and run the **Cook** cycle for **8 to 10 minutes** until steaming hot. Hit cancel as soon as the cheese is re-melted.
