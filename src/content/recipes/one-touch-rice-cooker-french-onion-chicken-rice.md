# One-Touch Rice Cooker French Onion Chicken Rice

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Standard rice cookers automatically regulate temperature by detecting when free-standing liquid is absorbed and the internal heat rises past **212°F (100°C)**. By layering the chicken on top of the seasoned grains, the meat steams gently from the rising broth while rendering its fat straight down into the rice. Using a dry French onion soup mix concentrates deep caramelized onion flavor and umami without needing to stand over a stove stirring onions for 45 minutes.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Rice Cooker Capacity:** Use a standard 6-cup (uncooked) capacity rice cooker or larger. Smaller 3-cup mini models will overcrowd, leaving the rice unevenly cooked.
- **Rinse the Rice:** Wash the grains in a mesh strainer until the water runs mostly clear. Skipping this leaves excess surface starch in the pot, which turns the rice gummy and scorches the bottom.
- **Do Not Stir Before Cooking:** Once the liquid and rice are set, lay the chicken pieces flat across the top. If you stir the raw chicken into the rice bed, it blocks liquid circulation and leaves you with crunchy, raw rice pockets.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Long-Grain White Jasmine Rice:** 2 cups (380g) – rinsed and drained. Do not use parboiled or brown rice; the liquid ratios will fail.
- **Low-Sodium Beef Broth:** 2 cups (480ml) – use low-sodium to control saltiness.
- **Dry French Onion Soup Mix:** 1 packet (1 oz / 28g) – store brand works just as well as name-brand.
- **Boneless, Skinless Chicken Thighs:** 1.25 lbs (560g) – cut into 1.5-inch bite-sized chunks. Don't waste money on chicken breasts; thighs stay juicy under steam.
- **Unsalted Butter:** 2 tbsp (28g) – cut into small pats.
- **Worcestershire Sauce:** 1 tbsp (15ml) – adds acid and depth.
- **Black Pepper:** ½ tsp – freshly ground.
- **Swiss or Gruyère Cheese:** 1 cup (100g), shredded – melt over the top at the end.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Rinse and Prep**: Rinse 2 cups of Jasmine rice under cold water until clear. Drain thoroughly so you don't throw off the liquid ratio. Pat the cut chicken thigh chunks dry with a paper towel and toss them with ½ tsp black pepper.
2. **Build the Base**: Dump the rinsed rice into the rice cooker inner pot. Pour in 2 cups of low-sodium beef broth and 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce. Sprinkle the dry French onion soup mix evenly over the liquid. Stir gently with a silicone spatula just until the soup powder dissolves into the liquid.
3. **Layer the Fat and Protein**: Drop the 2 tbsp of butter pats over the rice surface. Lay the seasoned chicken pieces in a single, flat layer directly on top of the rice. **Do not stir.**
4. **Run the Cook Cycle**: Close the lid and press the standard **"White Rice"** or **"Cook"** setting. Let the cycle run completely, which takes roughly **30 to 35 minutes** depending on your unit. Do not open the lid during cooking.
5. **Verify and Rest**: When the machine flips to "Warm", pop the lid. Check the thickest chicken chunk with an instant-read probe to ensure it registers at least **165°F (74°C)**. 
6. **Melt the Cheese**: Immediately scatter 1 cup of shredded Swiss or Gruyère over the chicken. Close the lid and let it sit on the **"Warm"** setting for **5 minutes** until the cheese is completely melted and bubbly. Fluff gently around the chicken chunks and serve immediately.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **The Rice is Mushy**: You added too much liquid or didn't drain the rice well after rinsing. *Fix:* Next time, pull back 2 tablespoons of broth to account for chicken rendering. If it happens now, leave the lid off on the "Warm" setting for **7-10 minutes** to let excess steam evaporate.
- **The Dish is Overly Salty**: You used standard full-sodium beef broth combined with the soup packet. *Fix:* Always use low-sodium broth or plain tap water. If the base turns out too salty, stir in ½ cup of frozen peas or corn during the final 5-minute rest to balance the sodium.
- **Raw or Crunchy Rice Patches**: Chicken was mixed into the rice instead of resting on top, trapping pockets of dry grain. *Fix:* Splash ¼ cup hot water over the dry spots, close the lid, and run a **"Quick Cook"** cycle for **8 minutes**.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **The Cheese:** Real Gruyère is expensive. Swap it for standard domestic Swiss, provolone, or low-moisture part-skim mozzarella. They melt just as well for half the cost.
- **The Broth:** Don't buy a carton of beef broth if you don't have it. Use 2 cups of tap water mixed with 1 teaspoon of beef bouillon paste (like Better Than Bouillon).
- **The Protein:** If chicken thighs are overpriced, use skinless bone-in thighs (add **5 minutes** to rest time) or drain a 15-oz can of chickpeas and drop them on top instead.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge:** Store leftovers in an airtight glass container for up to **4 days**. 
- **Reheat in the Rice Cooker:** Avoid the microwave; it makes the chicken rubbery and the rice dry. Add your leftover rice cooker chicken and rice back into the pot, drizzle with **2 tablespoons of water or broth**, close the lid, and hit the **"Warm"** or **"Steam"** setting for **10 minutes**. Stir once the steam starts escaping to redistribute the cheese and moisture.
