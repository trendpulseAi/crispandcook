# Crispy Air Fryer Lemon-Herb Chicken Meatballs

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Ground chicken dries out fast in a conventional oven and turns soggy when crowded into a skillet. The air fryer fixes both problems: intense, concentrated convection heat sets an exterior crust in minutes while sealing the steam and fat inside. The core trick here is working lemon zest and a splash of olive oil directly into the meat mixture rather than lemon juice. Juice adds excess acid and water that turns raw poultry mushy, while zest delivers pure lemon oil that browns cleanly under high heat.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Appliance specifics**: Tested in a standard 5.8-quart basket-style air fryer. If you have an oven-style air fryer with wire racks, use the middle rack position and place a drip tray on the bottom floor to catch drippings. Do not crowd the basket—leave at least 1/2-inch of space between meatballs so the circulating air can crisp the sides.
- **Damp hands are mandatory**: Ground chicken is much stickier than beef or pork. Keep a small bowl of cold water next to your prep board and dip your fingers between rolling each ball. This stops the meat from gluing to your skin and keeps the surface smooth.
- **Don't use 99% lean chicken breast**: Ultra-lean ground chicken breast turns into dry rubber pellets. Buy 92/8 or 93/7 ground chicken (a mix of white and dark meat). If you can only find 99% lean, add an extra tablespoon of olive oil to the mix to save the texture.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Ground chicken (92/8 or 93/7)**: 1 lb (450g) – Avoid 99% fat-free if possible.
- **Panko breadcrumbs**: 1/2 cup (25g) – Plain panko creates a lighter texture than dense, sandy Italian breadcrumbs.
- **Egg**: 1 large – Lightly beaten to bind the mix.
- **Lemon zest**: 1 tbsp (from 1 medium lemon) – Grate only the yellow skin; stop before hitting the bitter white pith. Save the naked lemon for squeezing over the cooked meatballs.
- **Garlic**: 3 cloves – Grated or finely minced. Don't use chunky cuts or they won't cook through in 12 minutes.
- **Dried oregano**: 1 tsp – Rub it between your palms before adding to wake up the oils.
- **Fresh parsley**: 2 tbsp (finely chopped) – Or substitute 1 tsp dried parsley.
- **Crumbled feta cheese**: 1/3 cup (50g) – Optional, but adds pockets of salty moisture.
- **Kosher salt**: 1 tsp – Use 3/4 tsp if your feta is exceptionally salty.
- **Black pepper**: 1/2 tsp – Freshly cracked.
- **Olive oil**: 1 tbsp – Keeps lean poultry juicy, plus cooking spray for the basket.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Preheat and Mix**: Set your air fryer to **380°F (193°C)** and preheat for **3 minutes**. In a large bowl, combine the ground chicken, panko, beaten egg, lemon zest, minced garlic, oregano, parsley, feta, kosher salt, black pepper, and 1 tablespoon of olive oil. Mix gently with a fork or your hands just until combined. Stop mixing as soon as the streaks of egg disappear—overworking ground poultry makes meatballs dense and tough.

2. **Portion and Shape**: Lightly wet your hands with cold water. Scoop out rounded 2-tablespoon portions (about 1.25 oz / 35g each) and gently roll them into 12 to 14 uniform balls. Place them on a clean plate or cutting board as you go. 

3. **Load the Basket**: Lightly mist the air fryer basket with high-smoke-point oil spray (avocado or canola; avoid aerosol sprays with propellants that degrade non-stick coatings). Arrange the meatballs in a single layer with space between each piece. If your basket is under 4 quarts, cook in two separate batches.

4. **Cook**: Air fry at **380°F (193°C)** for **12 minutes** total.

5. **The Mid-Point Shake**: At the **7-minute mark**, pull the basket out and give it a firm shake, or use silicone tongs to roll each meatball over. This redistributes the rendered fat and ensures the underside browns evenly.

6. **Finish Cue**: The meatballs are done when the exteriors are golden brown, sizzling, and firm to a light poke. An instant-read thermometer inserted into the thickest meatball must read at least **165°F (74°C)**. Transfer immediately to a plate to stop the cooking; leaving them in the hot basket will dry them out.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **The meatballs stick and tear when flipped**: This happens when the basket isn't preheated or lacks oil. Make sure the unit is hot before loading, and mist the basket surface. If a meatball feels stuck at minute 7, don't force it—give it another minute of heat to naturally release from the metal.
- **The meat is rubbery and dry inside**: You either cooked past **165°F (74°C)** or bought 99% lean chicken breast. Pull them the second your probe hits the target temperature, and stick to standard 92/8 ground chicken.
- **The meatballs flattened out into mini burgers**: Your mixture was too warm or wet. Warm fat softens rapidly at room temp. If your kitchen is hot, slide the shaped meatballs into the fridge for **10 minutes** before air frying so they hold their spherical shape under the fan.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Ground Chicken**: Ground turkey (93/7) works at the exact same temperature and timing. Ground pork is also cheap and yields even juicier results with zero changes to the method.
- **Panko Breadcrumbs**: Don't buy a new canister if you're out. Use 1/2 cup of crushed saltines, plain rolled oats blitzed coarsely in a blender, or plain sandwich bread whizzed into crumbs.
- **Feta Cheese**: If feta is too expensive, omit it entirely and increase the kosher salt by 1/4 teaspoon. Alternatively, toss in 1/4 cup of grated parmesan or pecorino from the shaker in your fridge door.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Let the meatballs cool completely, then store in an airtight glass or plastic container for up to **4 days**. 
- **Reheat in the Air Fryer**: Never microwave these—it turns the crust soggy and makes poultry taste gamey. Preheat your air fryer to **350°F (175°C)**. Toss the cold meatballs into the basket and cook for **3 to 4 minutes** until the skin sizzles again and the center is hot.
