# Crispy Air Fryer Lemon-Herb Chicken Meatballs

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Ground chicken dries out into rubber if you bake it in a conventional oven. The air fryer fixes this because the high-speed convection fan blasts the exterior with dry heat, crisping the outer layer before the inside loses moisture. The real trick here is grating the onion instead of chopping it; the raw onion juice hydrates the breadcrumbs, creating a panade that guarantees these air fryer greek lemon chicken meatballs stay tender even with lean poultry.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Appliance**: A standard 4-to-6-quart basket-style air fryer works best. If you have an oven-style air fryer, use the middle rack position so the top elements don't scorch the meat before the center cooks through.
- **Damp hands are non-negotiable**: Ground chicken is wet and sticky. Keep a small bowl of cold water next to your prep station to dip your fingers in before shaping each ball, or you will end up wearing half the mixture.
- **Preheating matters here**: Don't drop cold poultry into a cold basket. A properly preheated basket sears the bottom immediately, preventing the meat from sticking to the wire grate and flattening out.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Ground chicken (92/8 or 93/7 blend)**: 450g (1 lb) – Avoid 99% fat-free breast meat if you can; it turns chalky. If ultra-lean is all you have, mix in 1 tablespoon of olive oil.
- **Panko breadcrumbs**: 40g (1/2 cup) – Panko gives a lighter crumb than standard breadcrumbs.
- **Yellow onion**: 1/2 medium – Grated on the large holes of a box grater so you get both pulp and liquid.
- **Garlic cloves**: 3 cloves – Finely minced or grated on a microplane.
- **Egg**: 1 large – Lightly beaten to bind the mixture.
- **Lemon zest**: 1 tbsp – Zest from 1 large lemon. Save the juice for serving.
- **Dried oregano**: 1 tsp – Standard grocery-store Mediterranean oregano.
- **Fresh parsley**: 15g (2 tbsp), finely chopped – Stems removed.
- **Kosher salt**: 1 tsp – Use 3/4 tsp if using fine table salt.
- **Black pepper**: 1/2 tsp – Freshly cracked.
- **Feta cheese (optional)**: 40g (1/3 cup), finely crumbled – Adds pockets of brine and fat.
- **Olive oil spray**: For coating the basket and meatballs.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Preheat the Air Fryer**: Set your air fryer to **400°F (200°C)** and let it run empty for **4 minutes**.
2. **Build the Binder**: In a large mixing bowl, combine the grated onion (along with its juices), 40g panko breadcrumbs, beaten egg, minced garlic, lemon zest, oregano, chopped parsley, salt, and black pepper. Stir and let sit for **2 minutes** until the breadcrumbs absorb the wet ingredients and form a paste.
3. **Incorporate the Chicken**: Add the ground chicken and crumbled feta to the bowl. Use your hands or a fork to gently fold the meat into the breadcrumb mixture until just combined. **Stop mixing the second you stop seeing dry pockets**; overworking ground chicken turns the final texture dense and rubbery.
4. **Shape**: Wet your hands with cold water. Scoop out rounded portions (about 2 tablespoons / 30g each) and gently roll them into 1.5-inch balls. You should get roughly 14 to 16 meatballs.
5. **Load the Basket**: Lightly mist the hot air fryer basket with olive oil spray. Place the meatballs inside in a single layer, leaving at least a half-inch of space around each one so air circulates. If using a smaller air fryer, cook in two batches. Spray the tops with a light coat of olive oil.
6. **Air Fry**: Cook at **400°F (200°C)** for **10 to 12 minutes**. 
7. **Mid-Point Check**: At the **6-minute mark**, pull the basket out and give it a firm shake or use silicone-tipped tongs to gently flip each meatball. The bottoms should be set and lightly browned; flipping guarantees an even, golden crust all around.
8. **Finish Cue**: The meatballs are done when the exterior is deeply golden brown with crisp edges and an instant-read thermometer inserted into the center reads **165°F (74°C)**. Rest for **3 minutes** in the basket before serving so the juices settle.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **The meatballs stick and tear when flipping**: This happens when the basket wasn't hot enough or you used cooking sprays containing aerosol propellants (like Pam), which leave a gummy residue on non-stick coatings. Always preheat the basket, use pure oil in a pump mister, and let the meatballs cook undisturbed for at least **6 minutes** until a natural crust forms and releases itself from the metal.
- **They turn out tough and dry**: You either bought 99% fat-free chicken breast meat, overmixed the raw blend, or overcooked them past 165°F. Pull them the second your probe hits the safe temp. If you must use ultra-lean meat, add a tablespoon of olive oil or Greek yogurt to the raw mix to replace missing fat.
- **Soggy, pale meatballs**: You overcrowded the basket. If the meatballs touch each other, they trap moisture and steam instead of frying. Cook in two batches if your basket is under 5 quarts.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Ground Turkey**: Swap ground turkey (85/15 or 93/7) in a 1:1 ratio. It behaves almost identically to ground chicken and is often a dollar or two cheaper per pound.
- **Crushed Crackers for Panko**: Don't buy panko just for this recipe if you have regular saltines or Ritz crackers. Crush them finely; they absorb moisture just as well.
- **Dried Herbs for Fresh**: If fresh parsley is limp or expensive, skip it. Increase the dried oregano to **1.5 tsp** and add **1/2 tsp of dried dill or mint** directly from the spice rack.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Store leftover cooked meatballs in an airtight glass or plastic container for up to **4 days**. 
- **Reheat in the Air Fryer**: Do not microwave these unless you want a soggy, rubbery mess. Preheat the air fryer to **350°F (175°C)**. Place the refrigerated meatballs in the basket and heat for **3 to 4 minutes** until the internal temperature is warm and the surface crisps back up.
