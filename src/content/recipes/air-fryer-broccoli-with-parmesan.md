steps:
- title: Prepare the broccoli
  text: Wash the broccoli and dry it well. Cut it into similarly sized florets; pieces around 1 to 1½ inches (2.5 to 4 cm) across are a useful starting point.
- title: Season
  text: Place the florets in a bowl. Add the olive oil, garlic powder, salt, and pepper, then toss until the broccoli is lightly and evenly coated.
- title: Arrange in the air fryer
  text: Preheat the air fryer to 375°F (190°C) if your appliance recommends it. Arrange the broccoli in a single layer with a little space between pieces. Cook in batches if the basket is crowded.
- title: Cook until tender
  text: Air fry for about 8 to 10 minutes, shaking or turning the florets halfway through. Check early because small florets and different air fryers can brown quickly. The edges should be lightly browned and the stems tender when pierced.
- title: Add Parmesan and serve
  text: Transfer the broccoli to a serving dish and sprinkle with Parmesan while warm. Add a squeeze of lemon if desired and serve.
proTips:
- Dry the broccoli after washing; extra moisture can cause the florets to steam rather than brown.
- Cut the pieces to a similar size and avoid overcrowding the basket.
- Check the smallest florets first to prevent their edges from burning.
- Serve alongside chicken, fish, rice, or a baked potato.
storage: 'Refrigerate leftover broccoli in a shallow, covered container within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the temperature is above 90°F (32°C). Keep the refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below and use the leftovers within 3 to 4 days.


  To reheat, spread the broccoli in the air fryer or use an oven. Heat leftovers to 165°F (74°C), checking with a food thermometer where practical. The florets may become softer than when freshly cooked.


  Cooked broccoli can be frozen in a freezer-safe container, although its texture may soften further after thawing. Thaw it in the refrigerator and reheat thoroughly before serving.'
faqs:
- q: Can I use frozen broccoli?
  a: You can, but it may release extra moisture and need a different cooking time. Cook from frozen in a single layer, check frequently, and do not expect the same texture as fresh broccoli.
- q: Why did my broccoli burn?
  a: Florets that are very small or too close to the heating element can brown quickly. Cut pieces evenly and check them early, adjusting the time for your appliance.
- q: Can I make this without Parmesan?
  a: Yes. Leave out the cheese or use a suitable dairy-free topping. Add it after cooking and adjust the seasoning to taste.

Air fryer broccoli makes a simple side dish when you want tender vegetables with lightly browned edges. Garlic seasoning adds a savory note, and a sprinkle of Parmesan finishes the dish without hiding the broccoli’s natural flavor.

The key is to dry the florets well and give them enough room in the basket. Broccoli can brown quickly, particularly when the pieces are small, so check it early and adjust the cooking time for your appliance.

Serve it with chicken, fish, or a baked potato, and add a squeeze of lemon if you enjoy a bright finish.
