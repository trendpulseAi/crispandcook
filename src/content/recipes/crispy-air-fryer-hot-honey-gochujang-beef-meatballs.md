# Crispy Air Fryer Hot Honey Gochujang Beef Meatballs

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Convection heat strips surface moisture fast, giving you a deep, browned exterior crust in 12 minutes without drying out the middle. Cooking the meatballs completely naked first is the trick: if you add glaze inside the basket, the honey scorches into bitter black carbon before the meat is cooked through. Toss the piping-hot meatballs in the sweet-heat gochujang glaze *after* they hit the plate so the crust stays crunchy and the sauce stays glossy.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Basket Clearance**: Use a standard 4-quart (or larger) air fryer. If you have a small round basket, cook these in two batches. Crowding makes the meatballs steam in their own fat instead of crisping.
- **Fat Ratio Matters**: Stick to 80/20 ground beef. Leaner beef (90/10 or 93/7) turns into dry, leathery golf balls under rapid air circulation.
- **Gentle Hands**: Do not knead or compress the meat mixture like bread dough. Mix just until ingredients disappear into the meat; overworking crushes the fat pockets and ruins the texture.

## Ingredients & Measurements

### The Meatballs
- **80/20 Ground Beef**: 1 lb (450g) – fresh, not frozen-thawed if possible to minimize extra moisture.
- **Panko Breadcrumbs**: 1/2 cup (30g) – panko creates a lighter texture than fine Italian breadcrumbs.
- **Egg**: 1 large – beaten lightly.
- **Garlic**: 3 cloves – finely minced or grated on a microplane.
- **Fresh Ginger**: 1 tsp (5g) – grated (swap for 1/2 tsp ground ginger if that's what you have).
- **Soy Sauce**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – regular or low-sodium.
- **Toasted Sesame Oil**: 1 tsp (5ml) – for aroma in the meat base.
- **Scallions**: 2 stalks – thinly sliced, white and green parts separated.
- **Kosher Salt**: 1/2 tsp (3g).
- **Black Pepper**: 1/4 tsp (1g) – freshly cracked.

### The Hot Honey Gochujang Glaze
- **Gochujang Paste**: 2 tbsp (35g) – standard Korean fermented chili paste.
- **Honey**: 2 tbsp (40g) – standard clover honey.
- **Rice Vinegar**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – cuts the thick sweetness of the honey.
- **Warm Water**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – loosens the paste so it coats smoothly.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Mix the Base**: In a large mixing bowl, lightly whisk the egg, soy sauce, sesame oil, garlic, ginger, salt, and pepper. Stir in the panko and the sliced white parts of the scallions. Let it sit for **2 minutes** so the crumbs hydrate.
2. **Shape Without Packing**: Add the ground beef. Using your fingers, break the meat apart and mix gently until combined. Roll into 16 even balls (about 1.25 inches wide, roughly the size of a golf ball).
3. **Preheat the Air Fryer**: Set your air fryer to **400°F (200°C)** for **3 minutes**. A hot basket prevents the bottom of the raw meatballs from sticking.
4. **Load the Basket**: Place the meatballs in a single layer with at least 1/2-inch of space around each ball. Do not let them touch. 
5. **Initial Air Fry**: Cook at **400°F (200°C)** for **8 minutes** undisturbed. Do not touch or shake the basket early, or the soft meat can break apart before the crust sets.
6. **Shake & Finish**: Pull the basket out and give it a firm, quick shake to rotate the meatballs. Cook for an additional **3 to 4 minutes** until the exterior is deeply browned, sizzles aggressively, and the internal temperature reads **165°F (74°C)** on a digital thermometer.
7. **Whisk the Glaze**: While the meatballs finish, whisk the gochujang, honey, rice vinegar, and warm water in a large heatproof mixing bowl until smooth and runny.
8. **Toss and Serve**: Transfer the blistering hot meatballs straight from the basket into the glaze. Toss gently for 30 seconds until fully lacquered. Dump onto a serving plate and garnish with the remaining green scallions.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)

- **The meatballs burned on the outside but are pink inside**: Your basket was placed too close to the top heating element, or your air fryer runs hot. Next time, drop the temp to **380°F (193°C)** and add 2 minutes to the cook time.
- **The glaze slid right off and pooled in the bowl**: The glaze had too much water, or the meatballs weren't hot enough when tossed. Dump the meatballs in immediately out of the basket; the residual surface heat instantly tightens the glaze into a sticky coating.
- **The meatballs flattened out into hockey pucks**: The mixture was too warm or too wet when rolled. If your kitchen is hot, chill the shaped meatballs in the freezer for **10 minutes** before dropping them into the preheated basket so they hold their shape.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions

- **Ground Beef Swap**: Use ground pork or ground chicken thighs (dark meat only). Ground pork gives an identical juicy texture and is often half the price of beef. Avoid lean chicken breast or 93/7 turkey—they dry out completely.
- **Gochujang Replacement**: Don't buy a whole tub of gochujang if you won't use it again. Mix 1.5 tbsp of Sriracha with 1/2 tbsp of dark brown sugar and 1 tsp of soy sauce. It provides the same balance of heat, sugar, and body.
- **Panko Replacement**: Use crushed saltine crackers or stale bread ground in a blender. Crackers add fat and salt, so pull back the added kosher salt by half.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions

- **Fridge**: Let leftovers cool fully, then store in an airtight container for up to **4 days**.
- **Reheat in the Air Fryer**: Do not use the microwave—it turns the glazed meatball rubbery and makes the sauce greasy. Reheat the meatballs in the air fryer at **350°F (175°C)** for **4 to 5 minutes**. Keep the heat at 350°F so the honey glaze warms and gets sticky again without burning.
