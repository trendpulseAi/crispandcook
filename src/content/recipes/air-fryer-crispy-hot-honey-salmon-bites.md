# Air Fryer Crispy Hot Honey Salmon Bites

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Cutting salmon into uniform 1-inch cubes maximizes the surface area, letting high-velocity air crisp every edge in **8 minutes** without overcooking the center. Tossing the cubes in a tiny amount of cornstarch creates a dry, thin barrier that locks natural juices inside and grabs onto the seasoning. The trick to avoiding burnt sugar is holding the hot honey glaze until the final two minutes of cooking, giving you sticky caramelization instead of charred, bitter honey.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Basket Space Matters**: A standard 4-quart or larger air fryer handles this recipe in one batch. If you have a compact 2-quart basket, cook in two batches. Crowding makes the fish steam instead of crisp.
- **Dry the Surface**: Pat the raw salmon aggressively with paper towels before slicing. Wet salmon creates steam in the basket, which ruins any chance of a crisp exterior.
- **Cut Uniform Cubes**: Aim for exact 1-inch (2.5 cm) cubes. If pieces are uneven, small chunks will turn into dry leather while large chunks remain raw inside.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Skinless salmon fillets**: 1.25 lbs / 570g – standard farm-raised Atlantic salmon works best here because its higher fat content prevents it from drying out.
- **Olive oil or avocado oil**: 1 tbsp / 15ml – enough to bind the starch and spices.
- **Cornstarch**: 1 tbsp / 8g – the key ingredient for creating a light, crispy crust.
- **Garlic powder**: 1 tsp / 3g – for base savory flavor.
- **Smoked paprika**: 1 tsp / 2g – adds color and subtle smoke without heat.
- **Kosher salt**: ½ tsp / 3g – adjust slightly down if using fine table salt.
- **Black pepper**: ¼ tsp / 1g – freshly cracked.
- **Honey**: 3 tbsp / 45ml – standard store-brand clover honey.
- **Hot sauce or Sriracha**: 1 to 2 tsp / 5–10ml – use Frank's, Cholula, or Sriracha depending on what is in your fridge.
- **Apple cider vinegar**: 1 tsp / 5ml – cuts through the heavy sweetness of the honey.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Cube and Prep**: Pat the salmon fillets completely dry. Slice into even 1-inch cubes. In a small bowl, whisk together the honey, hot sauce, and apple cider vinegar; set aside.
2. **Season and Coat**: Place salmon cubes in a medium mixing bowl. Drizzle with 1 tbsp oil, then sprinkle the cornstarch, garlic powder, smoked paprika, salt, and pepper over the top. Toss gently with a silicone spatula until the fish has a dull, matte coating with no dry pockets of white starch remaining.
3. **Preheat and Load**: Preheat your air fryer to **400°F (200°C)** for **3 minutes**. Lightly mist the basket with cooking oil spray (do not use aerosol cans like Pam that degrade non-stick coatings; use a pump spray). Place the salmon cubes into the basket in a single layer with at least a finger-width of space between each piece.
4. **First Cook Phase**: Cook at **400°F (200°C)** for **6 minutes** undisturbed. Do not shake the basket; let the bottom set and crisp.
5. **Glaze**: Pull the basket out at the 6-minute mark. The edges should look firm, opaque, and lightly browned. Spoon or brush roughly three-quarters of the hot honey mixture evenly over the top of each salmon bite.
6. **Caramelize and Finish**: Return the basket to the air fryer and cook at **400°F (200°C)** for an additional **2 minutes**. Watch the heat here so you don’t burn it. Pull the basket when the glaze is bubbling and mahogany brown. Internal temperature should read **135°F to 140°F (57°C to 60°C)**. Drizzle with the remaining glaze and serve immediately.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **The glaze burns and turns black**: Honey scorches quickly above 320°F (160°C). If you toss the fish in honey before cooking, it will char before the salmon cooks through. Fix: Only apply the hot honey glaze during the last 2 minutes of the cooking cycle.
- **The salmon is soft and soggy**: The air fryer basket was either overcrowded, or the fish wasn't dried before seasoning. Fix: Space the cubes so air moves freely between them, and use paper towels to remove all surface moisture before cutting.
- **The bites are dry and rubbery**: Wild salmon (like Sockeye or Coho) is very lean and cooks faster than Atlantic salmon, drying out if cooked for 8 minutes. Fix: If using wild-caught salmon, drop cook time to **5 minutes** initially, glaze, and finish with **1 to 2 minutes**. Pull them off the heat the moment they hit **135°F (57°C)**.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Don't buy bottled hot honey**: Specialty hot honey runs $8 to $12 a bottle. Make your own in 10 seconds: combine 3 tbsp regular cheap honey, 1 tsp hot sauce, and a pinch of crushed red pepper flakes.
- **Salmon alternative**: Steelhead trout is frequently on sale for $2 to $3 less per pound than Atlantic salmon. It has similar fat levels, identical texture, and cooks in the exact same time.
- **Cornstarch swap**: Potato starch or tapioca starch work at a 1:1 ratio. If you have neither, standard all-purpose flour works, but the crust will be slightly softer rather than crisp.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Store leftover bites in a sealed glass container for up to **3 days**. 
- **Reheat in the Air Fryer**: Never microwave these—microwaving makes the salmon rubbery and turns the glaze into a watery mess. Preheat your air fryer to **350°F (175°C)**. Place the cold bites in the basket and heat for **3 to 4 minutes** until warm in the center and sizzling on the edges.
