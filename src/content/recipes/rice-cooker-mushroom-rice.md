steps:
- title: Check capacity and settings
  text: Use a cooker whose manual permits rice with vegetables and seasonings, and confirm that 1 US cup of dry rice meets its minimum quantity. Do not exceed its mixed-rice capacity or maximum fill line.
- title: Rinse and measure
  text: Rinse the rice in a sieve until the water runs less cloudy. Drain well and place in the inner pot. Add the measured water, oil, garlic powder, and salt, then stir once. Do not add water to a marked line on top of the measured liquid.
- title: Layer the mushrooms
  text: Scatter thin mushroom slices evenly over the rice without stirring them down. Avoid adding extra mushrooms on the first batch because their moisture can make the rice softer.
- title: Run the cooking cycle
  text: Close the lid and select the white-rice or mixed-rice program recommended by your manual. Expect roughly 25 to 40 minutes of cooking, sometimes longer. Do not repeatedly lift the lid during the cycle.
- title: Rest and fluff
  text: Once the cycle completes, rest covered for 10 minutes if the manual allows. Check that the rice is tender and the mushrooms are cooked. If the rice remains hard, use your manufacturer’s finishing guidance rather than repeatedly forcing a new cycle. Fluff gently and fold in green onions.
proTips:
- The listed 40 minutes includes an approximate cooking cycle and 10-minute rest; actual cooker timing varies.
- Slice mushrooms about 1/8 inch (3 mm) thick so they cook evenly without sautéing.
- Expect tender steamed mushrooms, not the browned flavor or texture of pan-fried mushrooms.
- Measure consistently and adjust the liquid slightly on a future batch if your cooker produces overly soft rice.

- q: Do I need to sauté the mushrooms first?

- q: Why is my mushroom rice wet?

This mushroom rice is a simple side for a weeknight meal: rinse the rice, layer on the mushrooms, and let the cooker handle the steady heat. Green onions added at the end bring a fresh contrast to the earthy mushrooms.

There is no separate sautéing step, so the mushrooms stay tender rather than browned. Keep that in mind if you usually make a skillet pilaf.

Rice cookers differ in their liquid needs and capacity. Start with your manual’s guidance, use thin mushroom slices, and give the rice a short covered rest before fluffing.
