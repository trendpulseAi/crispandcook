steps:
  - title: "Rinse and Drain the Grains"
    text: "Place 1.5 cups of white rice into a fine-mesh sieve. Rinse vigorously under cold running water for 60 to 90 seconds until the water runs clear. Drain thoroughly for 2 minutes to remove excess water that could throw off your liquid ratios."
  - title: "Soften the Aromatics"
    text: "Set your rice cooker to the 'Sauté', 'Quick Cook', or standard 'White Rice' mode. Melt 2 tablespoons of butter in the inner pot (operating around 300°F / 150°C). Add the sliced yellow onion and 1/4 teaspoon of salt. Cook with the lid ajar for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onions are limp, translucent, and developing golden brown edges. Stir in the minced garlic and dried thyme, cooking for 60 seconds until fragrant."
  - title: "Layer Rice and Liquid"
    text: "Turn off or cancel the cycle briefly. Pour the rinsed rice evenly over the sautéed onions. Pour in 1.75 cups of beef broth and 1 tablespoon of Worcestershire sauce. Gently press the rice below the liquid line using a wooden or silicone spoon, but do not stir vigorously (keeping onions near the heat helps prevent scorching)."
  - title: "Season and Place the Chicken"
    text: "Pat the chicken thighs dry with a paper towel and season both sides with the remaining salt and cracked black pepper. Nestle the seasoned chicken thighs across the surface of the submerged rice in a single, uncrowded layer. Close the rice cooker lid securely."
  - title: "Run the Cook Cycle"
    text: "Set the rice cooker to the standard 'White Rice' or 'Regular' cycle (typically 30 to 35 minutes, operating at roughly 212°F / 100°C). Cook until the appliance automatically switches over to the 'Keep Warm' setting. Verify the thickest part of the chicken registers at least 165°F (74°C) with an instant-read thermometer."
  - title: "Rest and Melt the Cheese"
    text: "Quickly open the lid, scatter the shredded Gruyère evenly over the top of the chicken, and close the lid immediately. Let the unit sit undisturbed on the 'Keep Warm' cycle for 8 to 10 minutes. The residual trapped steam will melt the cheese into a luscious blanket while allowing the rice grains to set. Fluff surrounding rice with a paddle and serve hot."

Most home cooks treat their rice cooker as a one-trick pony reserved for plain side grains, but its tightly sealed lid and automated heat-monitoring sensor make it one of the most effective braising vessels in your kitchen. Inside that insulated chamber, heat distributes evenly around the entire circumference of the pot, creating constant, pressurized steam that breaks down connective poultry tissues without drying them out.

This one-pot French Onion Chicken and Rice leans into those engineering strengths to deliver bistro-level comfort on a grocery budget. By combining the sweetness of slow-softened onions, rich beef broth, and savory Worcestershire sauce, the cooking liquid transforms into a deeply concentrated jus. As the rice boils and steams, it absorbs every ounce of that savory broth along with the juices rendering from the seasoned chicken thighs above it.

There is no stovetop grease to wipe down, no watching pots to prevent boil-overs, and no mountain of cookware waiting by the sink. In under forty-five minutes of completely hands-off cooking, you get perfectly fluffed, deeply seasoned rice topped with fork-tender chicken and a molten layer of nutty cheese.
