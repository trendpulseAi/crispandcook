# Sticky Gochujang Air Fryer Salmon Bites

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Cutting salmon into 1-inch cubes drastically increases the surface area, letting high-velocity convection air crisp the edges while keeping the centers juicy. The core trick is holding back the glaze until the last 3 minutes of cooking. Gochujang and honey contain high amounts of sugar that will turn black and bitter if exposed to **400°F (200°C)** heat for the full duration. By par-cooking the fish first, you get charred, sticky edges without scorch marks or dry, rubbery salmon.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Air Fryer Basket Size**: You need a standard 4-quart or larger basket. The cubes must sit in a single layer with air gaps between them; if they touch, the fish will steam instead of crisp. Cook in two batches if your basket is small.
- **Dry the Salmon Bone-Dry**: Pat the salmon cubes thoroughly with paper towels before seasoning. Residual water creates a barrier that prevents crisping and dilutes the seasoning.
- **Basket Prep**: Do not use aerosol non-stick cooking sprays (like PAM)—the propellants degrade the non-stick coating of air fryer baskets over time. Rub the grate lightly with high-smoke-point neutral oil on a paper towel, or use perforated parchment paper designed for air fryers.

## Ingredients & Measurements

### The Salmon
- **Skinless salmon fillets**: 680g (1.5 lbs) – cut into even 1-inch (2.5 cm) cubes. Atlantic salmon works best because its higher fat content prevents it from drying out.
- **Neutral cooking oil**: 15ml (1 tbsp) – avocado, vegetable, or light olive oil.
- **Kosher salt**: 3g (1/2 tsp).
- **Black pepper**: 1g (1/4 tsp) – freshly ground.

### The Sticky Gochujang Glaze
- **Gochujang (Korean fermented chili paste)**: 32g (2 tbsp) – standard medium heat tub; do not use gochujang sauce or marinade, which are watered down with vinegar.
- **Honey**: 21g (1 tbsp) – provides the stickiness and balances the fermented funk.
- **Low-sodium soy sauce**: 15ml (1 tbsp) – standard soy sauce makes this too salty once reduced.
- **Toasted sesame oil**: 5ml (1 tsp).
- **Fresh garlic**: 2 cloves – finely grated or minced.
- **Fresh ginger**: 5g (1 tsp) – finely grated.
- **Water**: 15ml (1 tbsp) – just enough to thin the glaze so it coats the fish without clumping.

### Garnish (Optional)
- **Toasted sesame seeds**: 1 tsp.
- **Scallions**: 2 stalks – thinly sliced.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Preheat and Prep**: Preheat your air fryer to **400°F (200°C)** for **3 minutes**. While it heats, cut the salmon into uniform 1-inch cubes. Pat them completely dry with paper towels. Toss the cubes in a bowl with 1 tablespoon of neutral oil, kosher salt, and black pepper until evenly coated.
2. **Whisk the Glaze**: In a small bowl, whisk together the gochujang, honey, low-sodium soy sauce, toasted sesame oil, grated garlic, grated ginger, and water until smooth. Set aside half of the glaze in a small dipping dish for serving.
3. **First Cook (The Sear)**: Place the seasoned salmon cubes in the air fryer basket in a single layer, leaving at least 1/2-inch space between each piece. Cook at **400°F (200°C)** for **4 minutes** untouched. The exterior should look opaque and matte with slight browning on the top edges.
4. **Glaze and Caramelize**: Pull the basket out. Using a silicone pastry brush or spoon, generously coat the top and sides of each salmon bite with the remaining half of the glaze. 
5. **Finish**: Return the basket to the air fryer and cook at **400°F (200°C)** for **3 to 4 minutes**. Watch the heat here so you don’t burn it. Pull the salmon when the glaze is bubbling, tacky, and shows small dark charred spots on the edges. An instant-read thermometer inserted into the center of the largest cube should read **125°F to 130°F (52°C to 54°C)** for medium, or **135°F (57°C)** for well-done.
6. **Rest and Serve**: Let them rest in the basket for **1 minute** so the glaze sets, then transfer to a plate and top with sesame seeds and sliced scallions.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)

- **The Glaze Tastes Burnt and Bitter**: You put the glaze on at the start of cooking. Gochujang and honey burn fast under direct convection heat. Always cook the salmon plain for the first 4 minutes, then apply the glaze for the final 3 to 4 minutes only.
- **Salmon Turns Out Mushy and Watery**: The salmon cubes were either too wet when they went in, or the basket was overcrowded. Moisture trapped in a packed basket creates steam. Dry the fish aggressively with paper towels and cook in two batches if your basket is under 4 quarts.
- **Salmon Sticking and Tearing on the Grate**: Salmon is delicate and high in protein, which bonds to hot metal. Wipe the basket grates with a small amount of neutral oil right before dropping the fish in, and do not attempt to flip or move the cubes during the first 4 minutes of cooking.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions

- **Salmon Cut**: Don't waste money buying expensive wild King or Sockeye salmon for this recipe. Lean wild salmon dries out quickly in an air fryer. Buy standard, budget-friendly farmed Atlantic salmon or thawed frozen salmon portions—the higher fat content keeps the bites tender.
- **Gochujang Alternative**: If you don't have gochujang and don't want to buy a tub, mix 1.5 tablespoons of Sriracha with 1/2 teaspoon of brown sugar and 1/2 teaspoon of tomato paste. It won't have the same deep fermented funk, but it provides the necessary heat, body, and acidity.
- **Fresh Aromatics**: Don't buy fresh ginger roots and garlic bulbs just for this. Swap the fresh garlic and ginger for 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder and 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger whisked directly into the sauce.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions

- **Fridge**: Store leftover salmon bites in a shallow, airtight glass container for up to **3 days**. Let them cool completely before sealing to prevent condensation from turning the glaze into water.
- **Reheat in the Air Fryer**: Do not microwave this dish; it will make the salmon rubbery, push out white albumin, and make your kitchen smell aggressively fishy. Preheat your air fryer to **350°F (175°C)**. Place the leftover bites on a small piece of foil or parchment paper and heat for **3 to 4 minutes** until warmed through and sticky again.
