steps:
- title: Check the manual
  text: Confirm that your appliance supports seasoned or mixed rice and that the amount meets its minimum and maximum quantities. Tomato mixtures can scorch in some basic cookers; do not use this method if your manufacturer advises against thick or acidic additions.
- title: Rinse the rice
  text: Rinse the rice in a fine-mesh sieve and drain thoroughly. Put it in the cooker’s inner pot.
- title: Dilute the tomato paste
  text: Whisk the tomato paste into the measured water until smooth. Add the oil, garlic powder, and salt. Pour over the rice and stir gently once to distribute. Use measured liquid or the manufacturer’s method, not both.
- title: Add onion and cook
  text: Scatter the very finely minced onion on top. Close the lid and select the mixed-rice or white-rice setting recommended by your manual. Cooking commonly takes about 25 to 40 minutes, but follow your machine rather than a fixed timer. Do not repeatedly open the lid.
- title: Rest and serve
  text: After the cycle finishes, rest covered for 10 minutes if the manual allows. Check that the rice is tender, fluff gently, and add optional parsley. If the bottom has browned, lift out the rice above it rather than scraping any burnt layer into the dish.

Tomato paste, a little onion, and garlic turn plain white rice into an easy side dish. Serve it with beans, grilled chicken, or vegetables for a meal that does not need a second saucepan just for the rice.

The flavor is mild and tomato-forward rather than spicy. Whisking the paste into the water before it goes into the cooker helps avoid concentrated clumps.

Use a rice cooker that permits mixed rice and tomato-based additions. Thick tomato solids can catch on the bottom of some machines, so the appliance instructions matter more than a one-size-fits-all cooking time.
