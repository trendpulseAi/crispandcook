# Air Fryer 20-Minute Crispy Pork Belly Bites

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Pork belly usually takes hours in a low oven to render out fat before you can crisp the exterior under a broiler. In an air fryer, high-velocity circulating air strips surface moisture while continuously melting the fat, letting it drain through the perforated basket instead of pooling. Cutting the meat into small 3/4-inch cubes exposes maximum surface area to this direct heat, yielding blistered, crackly edges and a tender interior in **20 minutes**. Adding a pinch of baking powder shifts the surface pH, speeding up the Maillard reaction without an overnight cure.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Appliance Sizing**: Use a 4-quart or larger air fryer. If the pieces touch or overlap, they will steam in their own fat rather than fry. Cook in two batches if your basket is small.
- **Dry Surface**: Press the pork dry with paper towels until the surface is completely matte. Surface moisture turns to steam, which kills crispiness.
- **Uniform Cuts**: Cube the pork into even 3/4-inch pieces. Smaller bits will scorch into hard rock pebbles before the fat renders on larger chunks.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Pork belly (skinless)**: 1.5 lbs (680g) – Buy whole slabs from an Asian market or local butcher; pre-sliced strips are usually cut too thin for hearty bite-sized cubes.
- **Baking powder (aluminum-free)**: 1 tsp (4g) – Do not use baking soda, or the pork will taste like metallic soap.
- **Kosher salt**: 1 tsp (5g) – Coarse salt grips the meat better than fine table salt.
- **Garlic powder**: 1 tsp (3g) – Standard grocery store brand works fine.
- **Smoked paprika**: 1/2 tsp (1.5g) – Adds color and woodsmoke depth without needing sugar.
- **Black pepper**: 1/2 tsp (1.2g) – Freshly cracked is best.
- **Chinese five-spice powder (optional)**: 1/4 tsp (0.5g) – Leave it out if you don't already have it in your spice rack.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Trim and Cube**: Pat the 1.5 lbs (680g) skinless pork belly thoroughly dry with paper towels. Slice the meat into uniform 3/4-inch cubes. 

2. **Season**: Drop the cubes into a dry mixing bowl. Sprinkle in 1 tsp baking powder, 1 tsp kosher salt, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1/2 tsp smoked paprika, 1/2 tsp black pepper, and optional five-spice. Toss vigorously with your hands until every piece is evenly coated with a pale, matte spice film. Do not add oil; pork belly provides its own cooking fat within 90 seconds.

3. **Load the Basket**: Dump the seasoned cubes into the ungreased air fryer basket. Spread them into a single layer with space between each piece.

4. **First Stage Cook**: Air fry at **400°F (200°C)** for **10 minutes**. The fat will start pooling in the bottom tray and the edges will turn pale golden.

5. **Mid-Point Shake**: Pull the basket out and shake it aggressively to rotate the cubes. If your air fryer tends to smoke from dripping pork fat, slide out the bottom tray and carefully pour the rendered liquid into a heatproof container before sliding it back in.

6. **Finish Crisp**: Return the basket to the air fryer and cook at **400°F (200°C)** for another **8 to 10 minutes**. 

7. **Finish Cue**: The pork belly bites are done when the edges are deep mahogany, the fat cap is bubbly and blistered, and a fork tapped against a cube sounds hollow and crunchy. Internal temperature should register between **195°F and 205°F (90°C–96°C)** with an instant-read thermometer, ensuring the internal collagen has fully softened. Dump onto a wire rack or paper-towel-lined plate and let rest for **2 minutes** before serving so the crust sets.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **Air Fryer Smokes Aggressively**: Pork belly dumps a massive amount of liquid grease into the bottom pan. At **400°F (200°C)**, that fat can hit its smoke point. **Fix**: Place a slice of cheap sandwich bread or 2 tablespoons of water in the bottom drawer beneath the crisper plate before cooking to absorb or cool the dripping grease.
- **The Bites Are Chewy, Not Crunchy**: You overcrowded the basket, trapping steam, or your pieces were cut too big. **Fix**: Never pile cubes on top of each other. If they don't fit in a single layer with breathing room, split them into two separate runs.
- **Burnt Rub With Unrendered Fat**: Using rubs with high sugar content (like brown sugar or sweet BBQ rubs) at high temperatures will cause the sugar to carbonize before the fat renders out. **Fix**: Stick to savory, dry spices during the cook. If you want a sweet or sticky glaze, toss the bites in the sauce in a clean bowl *after* they finish cooking.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Pork Shoulder (Pork Butt)**: If pork belly is too expensive or out of stock, use well-marbled boneless pork shoulder. Cut it into 1-inch cubes and toss with 1 tablespoon of neutral oil (since it has less natural fat). It won't be quite as rich, but it crisps well for half the price.
- **Cornstarch for Baking Powder**: If you don’t have aluminum-free baking powder, swap it for 1 teaspoon of cornstarch. You will lose the pH-blistering reaction, but the starch absorbs surface moisture and produces a clean, thin shell.
- **Store-Bought Seasoning**: Don’t waste money buying separate garlic powder, paprika, and spices if you have a container of lemon pepper, Cajun seasoning, or basic seasoned salt in your pantry. Just watch the salt levels and avoid sugar-heavy rubs.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Store completely cooled leftovers in an airtight glass or plastic container lined with a dry paper towel for up to **4 days**. The paper towel absorbs lingering grease and prevents condensation from softening the crust.
- **Reheat in the Air Fryer**: Never microwave cooked pork belly; the fat turns rubbery and the skin goes soggy. Dump chilled leftovers straight into the air fryer basket at **380°F (193°C)** for **3 to 4 minutes**. The ambient fat inside the meat will re-fry the surface back to its original crunch without overcooking the center.
