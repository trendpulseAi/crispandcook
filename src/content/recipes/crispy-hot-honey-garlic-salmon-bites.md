# Crispy Hot Honey Garlic Salmon Bites

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Whole salmon fillets often turn mushy on the bottom and dry on top before you get any decent caramelization. Cutting the fish into 1-inch cubes increases the surface area, letting the air fryer's convection fan rapidly crisp every side while keeping the interior tender. The trick is tossing the salmon in cornstarch and oil first, then applying the hot honey glaze in the final two minutes. If you put honey on at the start, the sugar scorches into a bitter, black mess before the fish is cooked through.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Appliance specifics**: A 4-quart or larger basket air fryer works best. If you have a smaller unit, cook in two batches; crowding causes the fish to steam instead of crisp. Leave out parchment paper here—it blocks the bottom airflow needed to crisp the underside of the bites.
- **Prep Tip 1 (Dry the fish completely)**: Pat the salmon aggressively with paper towels until the surface is bone-dry. Surface moisture kills crispiness immediately.
- **Prep Tip 2 (Uniform cuts)**: Cut cubes to an even 1-inch to 1.25-inch size. Remove the skin first by running a sharp chef's knife flat against the skin side while pulling the tail end taut. Uneven cubes mean small pieces turn into dry gravel while large pieces stay raw.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Salmon fillets**: 1.5 lbs (680g) – skinless, cut into 1-inch cubes. Standard Atlantic salmon works best because its higher fat content prevents it from drying out.
- **Olive oil or avocado oil**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – helps the seasonings adhere and promotes browning.
- **Cornstarch**: 1 tbsp (8g) – creates the light, crispy shell.
- **Garlic powder**: 1 tsp (3g) – seasons the crust directly.
- **Smoked paprika**: 1 tsp (2g) – adds color and subtle smoky depth.
- **Kosher salt**: 1/2 tsp (3g) – adjust if using fine table salt (cut to 1/4 tsp).
- **Black pepper**: 1/4 tsp (1g) – freshly cracked.
- **Honey**: 3 tbsp (45ml) – standard clover honey; do not waste expensive raw honey here.
- **Hot sauce or Sriracha**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – Frank’s RedHot, Cholula, or Sriracha all work.
- **Fresh garlic**: 2 cloves – finely minced (or 1/2 tsp garlic paste).
- **Soy sauce**: 1 tsp (5ml) – adds savory depth and cuts the honey's sweetness.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Prep Step**: Pat 1.5 lbs (680g) skinless salmon dry with paper towels. Cut into 1-inch cubes. Place cubes in a medium bowl, drizzle with 1 tbsp oil, then sprinkle with 1 tbsp cornstarch, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1/2 tsp kosher salt, and 1/4 tsp black pepper. Toss gently with a spatula until every cube has a chalky, matte coating and no loose powder remains at the bottom of the bowl.
2. **Cook Step**: Preheat your air fryer to **400°F (200°C)** for **3 minutes**. Arrange the seasoned salmon cubes in the basket in a single layer, leaving at least 1/2 inch of space between each piece. Set timer for **8 minutes**.
3. **Mid-Point Check & Sauce**: While the fish cooks, combine 3 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp hot sauce, 2 minced garlic cloves, and 1 tsp soy sauce in a small bowl. Microwave for **20 seconds** until runny and warm. At the **5-minute mark**, slide out the air fryer basket and shake it firmly to loosen and rotate the cubes. The edges should look opaque and slightly golden.
4. **Finish Cue**: At the **7-minute mark**, brush or spoon half of the warm honey glaze directly onto the tops of the cubes. Slide the basket back in to cook for the final **1 to 2 minutes** at **400°F (200°C)**. The glaze will bubble rapidly and turn glossy and sticky with slightly charred edges. The center of the thickest bite should register **135°F to 140°F (57°C to 60°C)** on an instant-read thermometer. Transfer bites to a clean bowl, drizzle with the remaining glaze, and serve immediately.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **Mistake 1: The glaze burns into a bitter black crust.** Honey burns at 320°F (160°C). If you toss the raw fish in honey at the start, you will ruin it. Only apply the sauce during the final 1 to 2 minutes so the sugars bubble without scorching.
- **Mistake 2: The salmon is pale, soft, and watery.** You either skipped patting the fish dry or you crowded the basket. Trapped steam ruins crispiness. If your air fryer basket is under 5 quarts, cook in two separate batches.
- **Mistake 3: The fish is tough, dry, and chalky.** Overcooking. Salmon continues to cook for a minute after leaving the heat. Pull the bites the moment they flake slightly under a fork; do not wait until white albumin heavily oozes out of the sides.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Salmon Swap**: Use steelhead trout instead. It looks and tastes almost identical to salmon, holds up equally well to high convection heat, and routinely costs $3 to $4 less per pound.
- **Hot Honey Swap**: Don't waste money buying pre-bottled specialty hot honey. Standard cheap store-brand honey mixed with hot sauce or 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes gives the exact same result for a fraction of the price.
- **Cornstarch Swap**: Use potato starch or arrowroot powder at a 1:1 ratio. If you have neither, all-purpose flour works, though the exterior will be slightly less crisp.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Store leftover bites in a sealed glass container for up to **3 days**. The moisture from the glaze will soften the exterior crust in the fridge.
- **Reheat in the Air Fryer**: Never microwave these—the fish turns rubbery and the crust turns soggy. To reheat, preheat your air fryer to **350°F (175°C)**. Place the cold salmon bites in the basket and heat for **3 to 4 minutes** until the exterior is sizzling, the glaze resets, and the interior is warmed through.
