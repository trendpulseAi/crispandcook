# Crispy Air Fryer Bang Bang Shrimp with Charred Broccoli

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
The secret to getting restaurant-crisp shrimp without a deep fryer is a dry cornstarch dredge combined with high-velocity air circulation. A standard pan leaves shrimp sitting in its own extruded juices, turning the coating to sludge; the air fryer basket forces moisture to evaporate instantly. Staggering the cooking by giving the broccoli a quick head start guarantees tender-crisp, charred florets right when the shrimp hits that tender, snappy finish. 

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Basket Capacity**: A 5.5-quart or larger basket handles this in one shot. If you have a compact 3-quart model, cook the broccoli first, set it aside, and fry the shrimp in two separate batches so you don't steam the crust.
- **Dry the Protein**: Raw shrimp are soaked in protective brine. Pat them aggressively dry with paper towels before dusting with cornstarch, or you will end up with a pasty, gummy shell.
- **Floret Size Matters**: Cut broccoli into small, bite-sized florets (about 1 inch). Large chunks will stay raw and woody inside while the tips turn to ash.

## Ingredients & Measurements

### The Shrimp & Broccoli
- **Raw shrimp**: 1 lb (450g) – peeled, deveined, tails removed (31/40 count works best; don't waste money on jumbo shrimp for this)
- **Broccoli**: 1 large head (approx. 10 oz / 280g) – cut into 1-inch florets
- **Cornstarch**: 3 tbsp (24g) – standard store brand is fine
- **Olive oil or neutral oil**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – for tossing the broccoli
- **Garlic powder**: ½ tsp
- **Fine salt & black pepper**: ½ tsp fine salt, ¼ tsp ground black pepper (divided)
- **High-heat cooking spray**: Avocado or canola oil (avoid propellant-laden aerosol cans that degrade non-stick baskets)

### The Bang Bang Sauce
- **Mayonnaise**: ⅓ cup (75g) – full-fat Duke's or Hellmann's holds the sauce structure best
- **Sweet chili sauce**: 3 tbsp (45ml) – standard Mae Ploy or grocery brand
- **Sriracha**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – adjust up or down depending on your heat tolerance
- **Honey**: 1 tsp (5ml) – balances the acidity

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Whisk the Sauce**: In a small bowl, stir together the mayonnaise, sweet chili sauce, sriracha, and honey until smooth. Set aside half of the sauce in a small dish for serving; keep the other half in a large mixing bowl for tossing the shrimp later.
2. **Season the Broccoli**: Toss the broccoli florets with 1 tablespoon of oil, ¼ teaspoon salt, and ¼ teaspoon garlic powder until evenly glossy. 
3. **Start the Broccoli**: Preheat your air fryer to **390°F (198°C)**. Dump the seasoned broccoli into the basket and cook for **4 minutes** until the edges just start to pick up faint brown spots.
4. **Dredge the Shrimp**: While the broccoli starts, pat the shrimp completely dry. Toss shrimp in a bowl with remaining ¼ teaspoon salt, ¼ teaspoon garlic powder, and the black pepper. Sprinkle the cornstarch over the shrimp and toss until completely coated in a powdery, matte layer. Shake off excess starch.
5. **Add Shrimp and Cook**: Pull the basket out, shove the broccoli to one side (or mix it through if your basket is wide), and lay the shrimp out in a single layer. Spray the tops of the shrimp generously with cooking oil spray until no dry, powdery white spots remain. Air fry at **390°F (198°C) for 7 to 8 minutes**.
6. **Shake at Midpoint**: At the **4-minute mark**, shake the basket vigorously or flip the shrimp with tongs. Give any lingering chalky starch spots another quick squirt of oil.
7. **Check Doneness**: The shrimp are done when the coating is crisp to the touch, opaque throughout, and reads **145°F (63°C)** internally. The broccoli edges should be deeply charred.
8. **Sauce and Serve Immediately**: Dump the hot shrimp directly into the large bowl with half the sauce. Toss rapidly for 15 seconds to coat. Plate alongside the charred broccoli and drizzle with the reserved sauce.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)

- **The coating is chalky and dry**: You didn't use enough oil spray on the cornstarch. Cornstarch needs fat to fry; pull the basket out immediately, mist the white powdery spots with oil, and cook for 1 more minute.
- **The shrimp turned rubbery**: You bought small shrimp and left them in too long, or cooked them past **145°F (63°C)**. Shrimp cook fast; check them at minute 6. When they curl into a loose "C" shape, pull them. If they curl into a tight "O", they are overdone.
- **Soggy shrimp after saucing**: You left the sauced shrimp sitting on the counter. The acid and moisture in mayonnaise dissolve crisp starch fast. Toss in the sauce seconds before the fork hits your mouth.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions

- **Protein Swap**: Boneless, skinless chicken breast or thighs cut into 1-inch nuggets work identically. Just increase the air-frying cook time to **10–12 minutes** until the chicken hits **165°F (74°C)**.
- **Mayonnaise Alternative**: Plain, full-fat Greek yogurt works if you want higher protein and fewer calories. It adds a slight tang, so add an extra half-teaspoon of honey to balance the acidity.
- **Veggie Swap**: Green beans or snap peas can replace the broccoli without changing the cook time or method. Skip dense root vegetables like carrots here; they won't cook fast enough.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions

- **Fridge**: Store leftover shrimp and broccoli in an airtight glass container for up to **3 days**. If you anticipate leftovers, do not toss all the shrimp in sauce—store sauce and shrimp separately.
- **Reheat in the Air Fryer**: Skip the microwave; it makes the shrimp rubbery and turns the coating into wet paste. Reheat unsauced shrimp and broccoli in the air fryer at **360°F (182°C) for 3 to 4 minutes** until warmed through and crisp, then spoon fresh sauce over top. If already sauced, reheat at **350°F (175°C) for 3 minutes**; the sauce will caramelize slightly.
