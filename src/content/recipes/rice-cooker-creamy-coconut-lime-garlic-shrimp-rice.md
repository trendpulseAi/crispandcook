# Rice Cooker Creamy Coconut Lime Garlic Shrimp & Rice

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Shrimp turns into rubber pencil erasers if cooked for the full cycle of a rice cooker. The fix is simple: cook the jasmine rice in a balanced mix of coconut milk and broth first, then drop the shrimp on top for the final **8 minutes** of residual steam. The steam gently poaches the shrimp to a tender snap while the rice absorbs the garlic, lime zest, and coconut fat below. One pot, zero stovetop grease splatter, and dinner hits the table in **35 minutes**.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Appliance**: Any standard 4-cup to 10-cup rice cooker (basic one-button toggle or fuzzy logic). If using a basic one-button cooker, do not lock the lid with force when adding the shrimp halfway through; just open, drop, and shut.
- **Rinse the rice thoroughly**: Wash your jasmine rice in cold water **3 to 4 times** until the water runs mostly clear. Coconut milk is dense and starchy; unrinsed rice will leave too much free surface starch, causing the bottom to burn and the top to turn to glue.
- **Dry the shrimp**: Thaw frozen shrimp completely, then press them firmly between paper towels. Wet shrimp release cold water that drops the internal steam temperature of your cooker and dilutes the sauce.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Jasmine rice**: 300g (1.5 US cups) – standard long-grain jasmine; avoid short-grain or sushi rice here.
- **Full-fat canned coconut milk**: 240ml (1 cup) – shake the can well before opening. Don't use "lite" coconut milk; it's just watered-down fat and leaves the rice flat.
- **Low-sodium chicken broth**: 240ml (1 cup) – balances the heavy coconut cream so the cooker's heating sensor doesn't shut off early.
- **Raw large shrimp**: 450g (1 lb), peeled and deveined (21/25 or 26/30 count) – tails on or off based on preference.
- **Garlic**: 4 cloves, minced (about 15g).
- **Fresh ginger**: 1 tsp (5g), finely grated or minced.
- **Fish sauce (or soy sauce)**: 1 tbsp (15ml) – adds savory saltiness to counteract the sweet coconut.
- **Lime**: 1 medium – zest the green skin into the rice pot; reserve the juice for finishing.
- **Kosher salt**: 1 tsp (6g).
- **Red pepper flakes**: 1/4 tsp – optional, for background heat.
- **Fresh cilantro**: 1/4 cup (10g), roughly chopped for serving.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Rinse and drain**: Place the **300g jasmine rice** in a fine-mesh strainer. Run cold tap water over it, swirling with your fingers until the cloudy water turns clear. Shake off all excess water thoroughly.
2. **Build the rice base**: Dump the drained rice directly into the inner pot of the rice cooker. Pour in **240ml coconut milk**, **240ml chicken broth**, **minced garlic**, **grated ginger**, **1 tbsp fish sauce**, **1 tsp kosher salt**, **1/4 tsp red pepper flakes**, and the **zest of 1 lime**. Stir gently with a silicone paddle to distribute the liquid evenly and ensure no grains stick above the waterline.
3. **Start the cycle**: Close the lid. Set your rice cooker to standard **White Rice** mode (or push the cook lever down). Let it run undisturbed for **18 minutes** (or until the liquid has mostly absorbed and small steam vents form across the surface of the rice).
4. **Prep the shrimp**: While the rice cooks, toss the dried shrimp in a bowl with a pinch of salt and black pepper.
5. **Add the shrimp**: Open the lid quickly at the **18-minute mark** (work fast to avoid losing heat). Lay the shrimp in an even, single layer directly across the hot surface of the rice. Do not stir them into the rice. 
6. **Steam to finish**: Shut the lid immediately. Let the cooker finish its cycle, or let it sit on the **Warm** setting for **7 to 8 minutes**. 
7. **Check doneness**: Open the lid and inspect the shrimp. They must be opaque pink with loose "C" shapes. If they look translucent or grayish, close the lid and let the trapped steam sit for another **2 to 3 minutes**. 
8. **Fluff and dress**: Squeeze the juice of the zested lime directly over the shrimp and rice. Scatter the chopped cilantro on top. Use a paddle to gently fold the shrimp and rice together from the bottom up, distributing the garlic coconut cream evenly. Serve hot.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **Rubbery, tough shrimp**: You tossed the shrimp in at the beginning of the cycle. Shrimp only needs **7 to 8 minutes** of steam heat. If you accidentally overcooked them, fold in extra lime juice and a splash of warm broth to soften the chew.
- **The bottom of the rice is scorched**: You used 100% coconut milk without cutting it with broth, or you skipped rinsing the rice. The sugars and heavy fats caramelized against the bottom heating element. If it smells slightly toasty, scrape around it; do not force-scrape the burnt layer into your fluffy rice.
- **Soggy, mushy rice**: You dumped soaking-wet thawed shrimp into the pot, dumping an extra 1/4 cup of water over the grains. Always blot shrimp bone-dry with paper towels before dropping them into the cooker.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Chicken broth swap**: Don't buy a whole carton of chicken broth just for this. Use **240ml water + 1/2 tsp bouillon powder** (or paste). Plain salted water works fine in a pinch.
- **Shrimp alternatives**: Frozen raw shrimp on sale is best, but you can swap in diced skinless chicken thighs (cut into 1/2-inch pieces). If using chicken, drop it in at the **start** with the rice, not at the end.
- **Cilantro hater**: Swap the cilantro for sliced scallions (green onions). They provide the same fresh, cut-through bite without the soapy taste.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Store leftovers in an airtight glass container for up to **2 days**. Cooked shrimp does not hold up well past 48 hours.
- **Reheat in the Rice Cooker**: Dump the cold rice and shrimp back into the cooker pot. Splash with **2 tbsp of water or broth** to create steam. Close the lid and press **Warm** for **10 to 12 minutes**. Stir once halfway through. Do not use the full "Cook" cycle or your shrimp will shrink and turn to rubber.
