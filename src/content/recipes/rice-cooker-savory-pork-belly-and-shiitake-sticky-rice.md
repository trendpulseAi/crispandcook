# Rice Cooker Savory Pork Belly and Shiitake Sticky Rice

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Traditional Chinese sticky rice (*lo mai gai* or *nuomi fan*) demands overnight soaking, pre-boiling, and multi-tier bamboo steamers. That is a weekend project, not a Tuesday night dinner. Your standard rice cooker traps steam at a precise, sealed temperature, which allows short-grain glutinous rice to cook through with far less liquid than open steaming. The core trick here is searing the pork belly first and tossing the dry, rinsed grains directly into the hot pork fat and soy reduction before adding liquid. This coats every individual starch grain, keeping the texture bouncy and chewy instead of collapsing into baby food.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Appliance**: Any standard 4-to-10 cup non-stick rice cooker. A simple one-button switch model works just as well as a fuzzy-logic machine. 
- **Rinse until water is clear**: Wash your glutinous rice in a fine-mesh strainer at least **3 times**. Surface starch is the enemy here; if you leave it on, it will settle to the heating plate and burn before the rice finishes cooking.
- **Save the mushroom water**: When you rehydrate your dried shiitakes, that dark brown soaking water is pure, free MSG. Strain out the grit and use it as your primary cooking liquid.

## Ingredients & Measurements
- **Glutinous Rice (Sweet Rice)**: 400g (2 US cups) – Look for bags explicitly labeled "sweet rice" or "glutinous rice." Do not use standard jasmine or basmati; they lack the amylopectin starch required for sticky rice.
- **Pork Belly**: 300g (10.5 oz) – Skin removed, cut into 1/4-inch thick bite-sized strips. Striated cuts with equal meat and fat work best.
- **Dried Shiitake Mushrooms**: 8 medium caps (approx. 25g / 0.9 oz) – Rehydrated and sliced thin.
- **Mushroom Soaking Liquid + Water**: Exactly 350ml (1.5 US cups) – Combined total volume.
- **Shallots**: 2 medium (approx. 60g / 2.1 oz) – Peeled and thinly sliced.
- **Garlic**: 3 cloves – Finely minced.
- **Regular Soy Sauce**: 30ml (2 tbsp) – Salty base; standard Lee Kum Kee or Kikkoman.
- **Dark Soy Sauce**: 15ml (1 tbsp) – Essential for that rich, glossy mahogany color.
- **Oyster Sauce**: 15ml (1 tbsp) – Adds sweetness and thick umami body.
- **Shaoxing Wine**: 15ml (1 tbsp) – Cuts the heavy pork fat.
- **Toasted Sesame Oil**: 5ml (1 tsp) – To finish after cooking.
- **Ground White Pepper**: 1/2 tsp – Sharp heat that pairs correctly with pork.
- **Scallions (Optional)**: 2 stalks – Thinly sliced for finishing.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Rehydrate and Rinse**: Place the dried shiitakes in **350ml (1.5 cups)** of hot water for **15 minutes** until soft through the stems. While soaking, rinse the sweet rice in cold water until the runoff is clear, then drain thoroughly in a sieve for **5 minutes**.
2. **Render the Pork**: Set a skillet over **medium-high heat** (or use the "Saute/Quick Cook" mode if your rice cooker allows lid-open searing). Add the sliced pork belly in a single layer. Fry for **4 to 5 minutes** until the fat renders out and the meat edges are crisp and golden brown.
3. **Sweat the Aromatics**: Lift the mushrooms out of the liquid (squeeze them dry over the bowl and reserve that broth). Toss the sliced mushrooms, shallots, and garlic into the hot pork fat. Fry for **2 minutes** until the shallots turn translucent. Watch the heat here so you don't burn the garlic.
4. **Glaze the Grains**: Pour in the regular soy sauce, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, Shaoxing wine, and white pepper. Stir for **30 seconds** until bubbling. Dump the drained, raw rice straight into the pan. Stir constantly for **2 minutes** over medium heat until all liquid is absorbed and the grains look glossy, brown, and translucent.
5. **Load the Cooker**: Transfer the coated rice and pork mixture into the rice cooker bowl. Pour the reserved mushroom soaking liquid over the top (pour it through a fine strainer to catch any sand from the mushrooms). Use a spatula to level the top flat. **Do not stir.**
6. **Run the Cycle**: Close the lid and hit the standard **White Rice** or **Cook** setting (runs roughly **35 to 40 minutes**). Leave the lid closed the entire time; do not peek and vent the steam.
7. **Rest and Fluff**: When the machine clicks to "Warm," leave it untouched for **10 minutes** to let the residual core steam finish setting the starches. Pop the lid, drizzle the sesame oil over the top, and fold the rice with a paddle using cutting motions from the bottom up.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)
- **Mistake 1: The rice is wet and mushy.** You used a standard 1:1 or 1:1.25 white rice water ratio. Glutinous rice absorbs far less water than regular long-grain rice. Stick strictly to the **350ml liquid for 400g rice** rule. If it happens, leave the rice cooker on "Warm" with the lid popped open an inch for **10 minutes** to cook off excess moisture.
- **Mistake 2: Hard, chalky centers in the grains.** You opened the lid mid-cycle, losing steam pressure, or used old rice. Fix it fast: splash **30ml (2 tbsp)** of boiling water over the top, shut the lid, and run another cooking cycle for **5 to 7 minutes**.
- **Mistake 3: The bottom layer is scorched black and bitter.** The sugar in the oyster sauce sank to the bottom heating zone. Always stir the sauces into the rice *before* transferring it to the pot, level it out gently, and never scrape the raw sauces directly against the bare bottom plate.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions
- **Pork Belly -> Bone-in, Skin-on Chicken Thighs**: Debone and dice two chicken thighs. Because chicken renders less fat than pork belly, add **10ml (2 tsp)** of neutral cooking oil to the pan when searing.
- **Dried Shiitakes -> Fresh Cremini Mushrooms**: Don't waste money buying imported dried mushrooms if you don't use them often. Dice fresh cremini mushrooms, sauté them until browned, and replace the soaking water with cheap boxed chicken broth.
- **Shaoxing Wine -> Dry Sherry**: Use an identical amount of cheap dry cooking sherry, or just use **15ml (1 tbsp)** of water mixed with a drop of rice vinegar.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions
- **Fridge**: Let the rice cool completely, then pack it into an airtight container. It keeps safely for **up to 4 days**. The rice will turn rock-hard in the cold as the starches firm up; this is completely normal.
- **Reheat in the Rice Cooker**: Never microwave sticky rice uncovered—it turns into rubber. Dump the cold leftovers back into the rice cooker pot, sprinkle **30ml (2 tbsp)** of tap water over the top, shut the lid, and hit the **Cook** cycle for **8 to 10 minutes** (or leave on **Keep Warm** for **15 minutes**). The trapped steam restores the soft, chewy bite without drying out the pork.
