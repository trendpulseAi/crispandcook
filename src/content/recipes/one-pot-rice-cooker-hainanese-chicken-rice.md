# One-Pot Rice Cooker Hainanese Chicken Rice

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Traditional Hainanese chicken rice requires poaching a whole bird, shocking it in ice water, and frying aromatics in rendered fat before simmering the rice separately. This one-pot method skips three dirty pots by cooking bone-in chicken thighs directly on top of the rice grains. As the rice cooker heats, the chicken fat renders downward, seasoning every grain with schmaltz, garlic, and ginger while the steam keeps the meat tender. The trapped moisture inside the cooker guarantees the chicken never dries out.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Rice Cooker Size**: Use a standard 4-cup to 10-cup rice cooker. A basic one-button switch model works just as well as an expensive fuzzy-logic machine.
- **Rinse the Rice**: Wash the jasmine rice in a mesh strainer until the water runs mostly clear, then shake out excess water thoroughly. Skipping this leaves too much surface starch in the pot, turning your rice into mushy glue.
- **Dry the Chicken**: Pat the chicken thighs bone-dry with paper towels before salting. Wet chicken releases watery runoff instead of rendering clean, aromatic fat into the grains.

## Ingredients & Measurements

### For the Pot
- **Bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs**: 4 pieces (approx. 680g / 1.5 lbs) – *Do not use skinless; the rendered skin fat is your primary cooking oil.*
- **Jasmine rice**: 400g / 2 US cups (uncooked) – *Standard long-grain white rice works, but jasmine gives the authentic floral aroma.*
- **Low-sodium chicken broth**: 475ml / 2 US cups – *Or water mixed with 1 tsp bouillon paste.*
- **Fresh ginger**: 25g / 1-inch piece – *Peeled and sliced into thin coins.*
- **Garlic**: 4 cloves – *Smashed and peeled.*
- **Toasted sesame oil**: 15ml / 1 tbsp – *Adds necessary nutty depth.*
- **Soy sauce**: 15ml / 1 tbsp – *Standard light soy sauce, not dark.*
- **Scallions (green onions)**: 3 whole stalks – *Cut into 2-inch lengths.*
- **Kosher salt**: 5g / 1 tsp (divided) – *Use half for the chicken skin, half for the rice liquid.*

### Quick Ginger-Scallion Sauce (Optional but Recommended)
- **Scallions**: 3 stalks – *Thinly sliced.*
- **Fresh ginger**: 15g / 1/2-inch piece – *Finely grated or minced.*
- **Neutral oil (canola or vegetable)**: 45ml / 3 tbsp.
- **Salt**: 1.5g / 1/4 tsp.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Season the Chicken**: Pat the chicken thighs dry with paper towels. Season both sides with **2.5g / 1/2 tsp kosher salt**. Let them sit at room temperature while prepping the rice.
2. **Layer the Grains and Aromatics**: Dump the rinsed, drained jasmine rice directly into the bottom of the inner rice cooker pot. Add the smashed garlic, sliced ginger coins, **15ml / 1 tbsp sesame oil**, **15ml / 1 tbsp soy sauce**, and the remaining **2.5g / 1/2 tsp kosher salt**.
3. **Add Liquid**: Pour in **475ml / 2 US cups of chicken broth**. Stir gently with a spoon just until the liquid, seasonings, and rice are level. Lay the 2-inch scallion segments across the surface of the rice.
4. **Place Chicken on Top**: Place the seasoned chicken thighs skin-side up directly on top of the rice and scallions in a single layer. **Do not submerge the chicken into the rice.** Keep the meat sitting on top so the skin steams cleanly without getting soggy underneath raw starch.
5. **Cook**: Close the lid. Set the machine to its standard **"White Rice"** or **"Cook"** cycle (typically **30 to 35 minutes**).
6. **Check Doneness**: When the cycle finishes, open the lid and insert an instant-read meat thermometer into the thickest part of the largest thigh (avoiding the bone). It must read at least **165°F / 74°C** (thighs are even better at **175°F / 80°C** for tenderness). 
7. **Rest and Serve**: Transfer the chicken to a cutting board and let it rest for **5 minutes** before slicing. Fluff the rice with a paddle, discard the cooked scallion segments and ginger slices, and serve hot with the sliced chicken and sauce.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)

- **The rice came out wet and gummy**: You didn't drain the rice well after washing, or you used boneless chicken that released excess water instead of rendering fat. *Fix:* Leave the cooker lid open on the "Keep Warm" setting for **5 to 8 minutes** to allow excess steam to evaporate, then fluff gently with a fork.
- **The chicken is pink near the bone when the timer dings**: The chicken pieces were too thick, straight out of a freezing fridge, or overlapping in the pot. *Fix:* Close the lid, switch the cooker back to "Cook" (or "Quick Cook"), and run it for an extra **8 to 10 minutes** until internal temperature hits **165°F / 74°C**.
- **The rice is burnt or scorched black on the bottom**: A light golden crust (tahdig/scorched rice) is normal and tasty, but bitter black rice means your broth had too much sugar or you added thick sweet soy sauce. *Fix:* Stick strictly to standard thin soy sauce and unsweetened broth in the bottom pot.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions

- **Bone-In Thighs vs. Boneless**: Bone-in skin-on is cheapest and delivers the best flavor. If using boneless, skinless thighs, drop the broth by **2 tbsp** and stir **1 tbsp of butter or neutral oil** into the dry rice so the grains don't turn out chalky.
- **Boxed Stock vs. Bouillon**: Don't waste money buying cartons of liquid stock. Dissolve **1 tsp of chicken bouillon powder (or Better Than Bouillon)** in **2 cups of warm tap water**. It's cheaper and tastes saltier and punchier.
- **Ginger Paste vs. Fresh Ginger**: Fresh ginger is best for clean flavor, but if you only have tube ginger paste, stir **1 tablespoon** directly into the broth before adding the chicken.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions

- **Fridge**: Store leftover chicken and rice together in an airtight glass container for up to **4 days**. The chicken fat will solidify on the rice grains; that is normal and will melt back down upon reheating.
- **Reheat in the Rice Cooker**: Dump the cold rice and chicken back into the inner pot. Splash **30ml / 2 tbsp of water or chicken broth** over the top to generate steam. Close the lid and press **"Warm" for 15 minutes** (or run a **"Quick Cook" cycle for 8 minutes**). The steam brings the rice back to its original fluffy texture without making the chicken rubbery like a microwave does.
