# One-Pot Hainanese Ginger Scallion Chicken Rice

## Why This Recipe Works (No BS)
Bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs render their natural fat directly down into the dry jasmine rice as the cooker boils and steams, seasoning every individual grain without requiring a pot of homemade stock. The enclosed steam chamber of a standard rice cooker traps moisture, meaning the meat cannot dry out even if left on the "Keep Warm" setting for an extra 15 minutes. The core trick is searing none of it beforehand: you place seasoned aromatics and raw chicken right over the washed grains and let the machine do the thermal regulation. While it cooks, you sizzle hot oil over minced ginger and green onions for the traditional condiment that carries the entire dish.

## Equipment & Prep Notes
- **Appliance specifics**: Any standard 4-cup to 10-cup rice cooker (both simple single-switch and fuzzy logic models work). If using a compact 3-cup cooker, scale the recipe down to two thighs; overcrowding prevents the steam from cooking the rice evenly.
- **Rinse the rice thoroughly**: Wash the jasmine rice in a mesh strainer until the water runs completely clear (at least 3 rinses). Excess surface starch combined with chicken fat turns rice into gummy paste.
- **Dry the chicken skin**: Pat the chicken thighs completely dry with paper towels before seasoning. Wet skin releases stagnant surface liquid that dilutes the cooking broth and turns the skin rubbery rather than meltingly tender.

## Ingredients & Measurements

### For the Rice & Chicken:
- **Bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs**: 1.5 lbs / 680g (4 medium thighs) – do not use chicken breasts; they will dry out and lack the fat needed to flavor the rice.
- **Jasmine rice**: 2 cups / 370g – standard long-grain white jasmine rice.
- **Low-sodium chicken broth**: 1 ¾ cups / 420ml – slightly less than the standard 1:1 ratio because the chicken releases roughly ¼ cup of its own juices.
- **Fresh ginger**: 1.5 tbsp / 20g – peeled and finely grated.
- **Garlic**: 4 cloves – finely minced or grated.
- **Toasted sesame oil**: 1 tsp / 5ml – for the rice base.
- **Kosher salt**: 1.5 tsp / 9g – divided (1 tsp for seasoning chicken, ½ tsp mixed into the rice).
- **Ground white pepper**: ½ tsp / 1g – black pepper works if that is all you have.

### For the Sizzled Ginger Scallion Sauce:
- **Scallions (green onions)**: 1 large bunch / approx. 1 cup / 75g – washed, dried, and thinly sliced (both green and white parts).
- **Fresh ginger**: 2 tbsp / 30g – peeled and finely minced.
- **Neutral high-heat oil**: ⅓ cup / 80ml – peanut, canola, or vegetable oil.
- **Kosher salt**: ½ tsp / 3g.
- **Granulated sugar**: ¼ tsp / 1g – balances the bite of raw ginger.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Season the chicken**: Pat the chicken thighs dry with paper towels. Rub all sides with **1 tsp kosher salt** and **½ tsp white pepper**. Set aside at room temperature while prepping the rice so the meat isn't refrigerator-cold when hitting the pot.
2. **Wash and drain the rice**: Rinse the 2 cups of jasmine rice in cold water until the water runs clear. Drain thoroughly in a fine-mesh sieve for **2 minutes** to remove trapped tap water.
3. **Load the rice cooker**: Dump the drained rice into the inner pot. Add the grated ginger, minced garlic, sesame oil, and remaining **½ tsp kosher salt**. Pour in **1 ¾ cups low-sodium chicken broth**. Stir gently to distribute the aromatics and level the rice into a flat, even layer.
4. **Position the meat**: Lay the chicken thighs directly on top of the flat rice layer, skin-side up. Do not submerge the chicken into the liquid; let it rest directly on the surface of the rice.
5. **Run the cycle**: Close the lid and press the standard **"White Rice" or "Cook"** button. The cycle will take approximately **30 to 35 minutes**. Do not open the lid while the cooker is running.
6. **Make the sauce**: While the rice cooks, place the sliced scallions, minced ginger, ½ tsp salt, and ¼ tsp sugar into a heatproof ceramic or metal bowl. In a small skillet, heat ⅓ cup of neutral oil over medium-high until it shimmers and shows faint wisps of smoke (around **350°F / 175°C**). Pour the hot oil directly over the scallion mixture. It will violently bubble and sizzle—stir immediately with a spoon to ensure even cooking. Set aside.
7. **Check doneness**: When the cook cycle finishes, open the lid and insert an instant-read thermometer into the thickest part of the largest thigh (avoiding the bone). It must read at least **165°F (74°C)**; thighs are best around **175°F–185°F (79°C–85°C)**. 
8. **Rest and fluff**: Transfer the cooked chicken to a cutting board. Close the cooker lid and let the rice rest undisturbed on the "Keep Warm" setting for **5 minutes**. Slice the chicken, fluff the seasoned rice with a paddle, and serve immediately with generous spoonfuls of the ginger scallion sauce over top.

## What Can Go Wrong (And How to Fix It)

- **The rice comes out wet and mushy**: You either used too much broth or failed to drain the rinsed rice. The chicken drops significant fat and water as it cooks. Next time, drop the broth down by 2–3 tablespoons. To salvage a wet batch right now: remove the chicken, leave the rice cooker lid open on the "Keep Warm" setting for **10 minutes**, and gently turn the rice with a spatula to let excess steam vent off.
- **The chicken is pink near the bone**: Placing ice-cold chicken into the pot slows down heat penetration. If the internal temperature reads below **165°F (74°C)** when the cycle clicks off, keep the lid shut and reset the cooker on a quick-cook cycle or leave it on "Keep Warm" for an additional **10 to 12 minutes**. Residual steam will finish cooking the meat.
- **The ginger scallion sauce tastes bitter or scorched**: The oil was heated past its smoke point, burning the delicate scallion greens on contact. Pull the oil off the stove the second you see ripples and a single faint wisp of smoke—do not wait until it fills the kitchen with haze.

## Cheap Swaps & Pantry Substitutions

- **Bone-in thighs for boneless**: You can use 1.25 lbs (560g) of boneless, skinless chicken thighs if they are on sale. Because they carry less fat and skin, reduce the broth to **1 ½ cups** and add 1 extra tablespoon of neutral oil to the rice so it doesn't cook dry.
- **Chicken broth for water + bouillon**: Don't buy an expensive carton of stock for this. Mix **1 ¾ cups of warm tap water** with **1 teaspoon of chicken bouillon powder** or Better Than Bouillon paste. 
- **White pepper for black pepper**: Authentic Hainanese profiles rely on ground white pepper for earthy heat without black flecks, but standard ground black pepper works fine at a 1:1 ratio.

## Storage & Reheating Instructions

- **Fridge**: Store leftover chicken, rice, and sauce in separate airtight glass containers for up to **4 days**. Keep the sauce chilled; the oil will solidify in the fridge, which is normal—bring it to room temperature for 10 minutes before serving.
- **Reheat in the Rice Cooker**: Dump cold rice and sliced chicken back into the rice cooker pot. Sprinkle **2 tablespoons of water** over the top to generate steam. Close the lid and press the standard "Cook" or "Reheat" cycle. Check after **8 to 10 minutes**; the appliance will heat the chicken through and re-steam the rice back to its original fluffy texture without drying out the grains. Avoid the microwave if possible, as it turns chicken fat rubbery and hardens cold rice.
