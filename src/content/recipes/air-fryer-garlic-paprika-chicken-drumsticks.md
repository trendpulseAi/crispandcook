  with: Use smoked paprika for a smokier flavor, or combine equal parts sweet and smoked paprika.

steps:
- title: Prepare the chicken
  text: Pat the drumsticks dry with paper towels. Drying the skin helps it brown more evenly. Wash your hands and clean any surfaces or utensils that touched the raw chicken.
- title: Mix the seasoning
  text: In a small bowl, combine the paprika, garlic powder, thyme, salt, and black pepper. Place the chicken in a separate bowl, add the oil and seasoning, and toss until the pieces are evenly coated.
- title: Arrange in the air fryer
  text: Preheat the air fryer to 380°F (193°C) if the manufacturer recommends preheating. Arrange the drumsticks in a single layer with room around each piece. If they do not fit without crowding, cook them in batches.
- title: Cook and turn
  text: Air fry for approximately 22 to 28 minutes, turning the drumsticks halfway through. Smaller pieces may cook sooner; larger pieces and different appliances can take longer.
- title: Check the temperature
  text: Insert an instant-read thermometer into the thickest part of each drumstick without touching the bone. The chicken is safe to eat when every piece reaches at least 165°F (74°C). Continue cooking and check again if necessary.
- title: Rest and serve
  text: Transfer the chicken to a clean plate and let it rest for about 5 minutes. Serve warm with optional lemon wedges and parsley.
proTips:
- Leave space around each drumstick. A crowded basket can prevent the hot air from reaching all sides.
- Use a thermometer rather than relying on skin color or the suggested cooking time.
- For a simple dinner, serve the chicken with baked potatoes, rice, or a crisp green salad.
- If you cook in batches, account for the extra time when planning the meal.
storage: 'Refrigerate leftover chicken in a shallow, covered container within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the temperature is above 90°F (32°C). Keep the refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below and use the leftovers within 3 to 4 days.


  For longer storage, freeze the chicken in airtight, freezer-safe containers or bags. For best quality, use within about 4 months. Thaw frozen leftovers in the refrigerator.


  Reheat in the air fryer or oven until the thickest part reaches 165°F (74°C). Check the temperature without touching the bone. The exact reheating time depends on the size of the drumsticks and your appliance.'
faqs:
- q: How do I know when the chicken drumsticks are cooked?
  a: Check the thickest part of each piece with a food thermometer, avoiding the bone. Every drumstick should reach at least 165°F (74°C). Cooking time is only a guide.
- q: Can I use frozen drumsticks?
  a: For the most even results with this recipe, thaw them in the refrigerator first. Frozen chicken needs different cooking times and must still reach 165°F (74°C).
- q: Why is the skin not as crisp as I expected?
  a: Moisture and overcrowding can prevent browning. Pat the chicken dry before seasoning and arrange it in a single layer with space between the pieces.

If you enjoy a comforting chicken dinner with a well-seasoned, golden exterior, these air fryer drumsticks are a simple recipe to add to your rotation. Garlic and paprika give the chicken a savory flavor, while the air fryer keeps the preparation straightforward.

The key is to leave space between the pieces and check the chicken with a thermometer. Drumsticks vary in size, so the suggested cooking time is a starting point rather than a guarantee.

Serve them with potatoes and a green salad for a hearty meal, or add rice and vegetables for an easy dinner. A squeeze of lemon brings a little freshness to the warm spices.
